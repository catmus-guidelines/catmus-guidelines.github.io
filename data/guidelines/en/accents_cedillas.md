### Accents

Some manuscripts, notably 12th-century Anglo-Norman manuscripts, may feature accented vowels. These accents indicate a 
phonetic alteration or hiatus, but noting them can lead to ambiguity. Indeed, how can we distinguish a dotted "i" from 
a distinguished "i" when the accent may designate palatalization, a hiatus, a tonic vowel or paleographic 
disambiguation? As these accents are relatively marginal, we invite transcribers not to note them. However, 
the phenomenon may be noted as an exception, depending on the specific problems encountered in a given corpus.

As far as Castilian corpora are concerned, Spanish accentuation appears in the 16th century and becomes normalized from 
the 18th century onwards. We recommend not accentuating texts prior to this period, but transcribing the accentuation 
*when it appears* in modern and contemporary manuscript and printed sources.

### Cedillas

Cedilla e's mark the latin diphthong ae, and are very common in Latin manuscripts from the X-XIIth centuries. 
We recommend transcribing them for medieval French manuscripts, to guarantee encoding consistency.
The combining character to be used is [U+0327] (Combining Cedilla).
